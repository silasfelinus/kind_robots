## WH-001 · The Human Statue Who Keeps Turning

**Location:** Arcata Plaza. **Scene vocabulary:** plaza. **Original canonical hook:** A busker freezes every time someone says the town's name, even when nobody is watching.

**Opening narration:** A busker freezes every time someone says the town's name, even when nobody is watching.

**Escalation:** The statue on Arcata Plaza cannot move whenever somebody says the town's name. His shadow can, and has just started collecting tips. 'I need a sandwich,' the busker tells you through clenched teeth. A tourist shouts ARCATA again to get a better video. The shadow holds up two fingers, either a peace sign or an invoice.

**Option A, investigate (visible 7 check):** Step into the chalk circle and ask who drew its boundary

- **Pass:** Under the chalk circle you uncover a municipal map marked for next Thursday, with a bus stop absent from today's streets. The shadow circles it with the toe of your shoe. 'That does not make him public property,' the busker says, accepting your photograph of the map but refusing another portrait.
- **Miss:** Your foot smears the circle and the chalk clings to your boots. The shadow follows you three embarrassing steps while the busker finally escapes. A tourist has a blurry picture, which they want to sell. You cannot prove what moved; you can refuse to be their publicity agent.

**Option B, assist without a check:** Get him offstage and stop the crowd filming without permission

- **Result:** You stand between the camera phones and a man who wants to be left alone. Somebody calls that censorship. The busker calls it lunch and finally eats. Behind you, the shadow finishes its last bow to an audience of exactly one stray dog.

**Location:** Arcata Plaza. **Scene vocabulary:** plaza. **Original canonical hook:** A ballot kiosk announces an unopposed mollusk; only the losing candidate can hear its platform.

**Opening narration:** A ballot kiosk announces an unopposed mollusk; only the losing candidate can hear its platform.

**Escalation:** An oyster has won a town election despite having no address, pulse or apparent interest in public office. The defeated candidate insists the voting machine is broken; the fishers would prefer to read the platform first.

**Option A, investigate (visible 7 check):** Ask for a translation and compare it with the actual ballot language

- **Pass:** The manifesto turns out to be a tide forecast written in the language of municipal ordinances. Next week's low water is on it. Next week's low water has not been scheduled.
- **Miss:** The translator admits they guessed at half the clauses. The oyster closes its shell and the defeated candidate reluctantly agrees the vote needs an audit.

**Option B, assist without a check:** Hold an open meeting where the affected shoreline users speak first

- **Result:** A wheelchair user, two fishers and the harbormaster agree on real access hours. The oyster abstains, quite possibly for tax reasons.

## WH-008 · Rocky Horror, Actual Horror

**Location:** Minor Theatre. **Scene vocabulary:** theater. **Original canonical hook:** A shadow-cast prop gains agency and demands better stage directions.

**Opening narration:** A shadow-cast prop gains agency and demands better stage directions.

**Escalation:** At the Minor Theatre fundraiser, an alien stage prop refuses the stabbing scene. The director says cancelling will cost the volunteer crew its rent money. 'What do you think the stabbing costs us?' the understudy asks, holding a prop knife with the safety catch broken. The alien is not speaking, but it has carefully underlined the injury clause in a union handbook.

**Option A, investigate (visible 7 check):** Ask the prop and cast to rehearse a version everyone can consent to

- **Pass:** The understudy rewrites the cue, the alien nods by tipping its painted antenna, and the scene works without a blade near anyone's throat. In exchange it hands the stage manager a warehouse address dated tomorrow. 'A useful tip is not permission to send an unpaid intern,' she says, and locks the script in her desk.
- **Miss:** The alien will not take its mark. The crowd laughs until the understudy shows them the old script, its dangerous direction signed by nobody who still works here. The director accuses you of sabotaging the fundraiser. You have not fixed opening night; you have helped the crew refuse a bad rehearsal.

**Option B, assist without a check:** Replace the dangerous effect and give the crew an actual say

- **Result:** You swap the stabbing for a collapsing paper moon and let the crew vote on the change. The audience laughs harder at the cheap moon than at last year's expensive blood effect. The director is furious about artistic compromise. The understudy counts the uninjured cast and calls it a standing ovation.
