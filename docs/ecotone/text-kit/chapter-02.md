## WH-022 · The Tide-Pool Tenant

**Location:** Moonstone Beach. **Scene vocabulary:** beach. **Original canonical hook:** A tiny landlord charges rent to every crab and makes the player mediator.

**Opening narration:** A tiny landlord charges rent to every crab and makes the player mediator.

**Escalation:** At low tide a crab landlord posts EVICTED notices on the borrowed shells of three hermit crabs. The rent is moonlight, collected in empty limpet shells, and a child has already fetched a bucket to rescue the smallest tenant. 'You can't make somebody a refugee to prove you're kind,' the tide-pool volunteer says, gently taking the bucket away. A bigger crab raises one claw at the word tenant, in either agreement or complaint.

**Option A, investigate (visible 7 check):** Read the lease terms and watch the animals from outside the pool

- **Pass:** The tiny rent ledger corresponds to a real drainage blockage upstream. The volunteer points out which channel is still getting fresh water, and the child draws it instead of moving the animals. The crab landlord claims to have filed the report months ago, then tries to charge a research fee. You have evidence of the drainage problem, not permission to rearrange the pool.
- **Miss:** The lease dissolves when the tide surges, taking your careful list of tenants with it. The child wants to scoop the crabs out before they are washed away. The volunteer stops both of you: this tide is their home, not your emergency. You cannot prove what the landlord charged, but a photograph of the dry upstream channel survives.

**Option B, assist without a check:** Keep hands out of the pool and help the child build a visitor sign

- **Result:** You help the child write a visitor sign that warns people to keep their hands out of the pool, even when the occupants appear to be arguing with their landlord. The child is disappointed not to save anybody personally. Then the smallest crab chooses a new shell by itself. The landlord stamps the sign APPROVED and sends the volunteer a bill for stationery.

## WH-023 · Message in a Glass Kombucha

**Location:** Moonstone Beach. **Scene vocabulary:** beach. **Original canonical hook:** A bottle washes ashore with the player's own handwriting and tomorrow's date.

**Opening narration:** A bottle washes ashore with the player's own handwriting and tomorrow's date.

**Escalation:** A kombucha bottle lands at your feet with a warning written in your handwriting. Tomorrow, it says, the cliff above three picnicking families will fall. 'Does the future always use my terrible cursive?' you ask. A parent says the children are finally asleep and refuses to move on the strength of a bottle. Pebbles tick from the overhang like someone clearing their throat.

**Option A, investigate (visible 7 check):** Compare the sketch with the cliff face and an independently posted warning

- **Pass:** You match the sketch to a new crack that the park notice omits. A local volunteer confirms fresh rockfall; the bottle's date remains unverified. When the family moves, one father asks whether you saved them with magic. 'With a second set of eyes,' you answer. The paper in the bottle signs itself again, less legibly.
- **Miss:** Rain dissolves the timestamp in your handwriting. You cannot tell the family tomorrow is the danger. A pebble hits your sleeve and you stop trying to win an argument with a letter; you warn them about the loose rock you can actually see. They may still decide you are wrong.

**Option B, assist without a check:** Move people away from the unstable edge without asking them to believe you

- **Result:** You carry blankets, sandwiches, and one sleeping toddler away from the overhang, giving the parents enough practical help to choose safety without swallowing your story. Minutes later stones strike the abandoned picnic spot. The father who doubted you hands back your borrowed blanket and says, 'I still don't believe in future mail.'
