    Four pilots. One pearlescent galaxy.

    This is the private flight deck, not the public Arcade release. Choose your player count in the standard Arcade cabinet and fly the Pinkwake Estuary through the Pearl Seahorse boss. After freeing its pod, the world and music bridge into Moonjelly Fields without resetting lives, weapons or charge. Act II introduces living jelly fleets, a stranded Nemi beacon, Lilt's warning, and the three-phase Aurelia Array. Each Act opens on its painted title card and runs eight authored sections of formations before its boss.

    Flight controls
    Use directional controls to steer and hold A to fire. Hold B for precise, slower movement and focused shots; a fully charged super activates on a fresh B press. B no longer gives a free shield refill. Destroy enemy scouts and collect P (power), S (shield), and star (super charge) modules. The cabinet manages keyboard, gamepad, local player seats, pause and touch controls. Ships are numbered as well as color-coded. Choose up to four local pilots on the cabinet. While paused, the player-count switch can add or bench local players without restarting the run. Returning pilots keep their lives and upgrades; pausing does not refill supplies. With an earned full charge, Solenne's Halo super can revive one fallen active teammate.

    Read the battlefield
    Pinkwake waves introduce picket scouts, spiral scouts, slower anchor mines and pearl urchins. Shoot hostile weapons while dodging their visible projectiles; graze bullets to build charge. The small central hitbox is shown in focus mode. A green SOS station appears mid-run; fly into it to rescue the crew and earn shield repairs. Before B01 Pearl Seahorse arrives, Lilt warns that it carries a living escape pod. Wait for boss targeting pulses, dodge the spread volleys, and destroy its weapons to liberate the pod. Its three health bands change the attacks.

    Moonjelly rescue
    Four new enemy behaviors appear in the jelly fields: glowing lantern fans, paired relay pellets, downward-skimming beads, and breakable coral drifters. A pale green NEMI beacon drifts through the mid-act waves; reach it before it passes to rescue her and earn super charge for every active pilot. The mid-Act Lilt conversation changes based on that rescue. Aurelia's three health bands open dangerous fan patterns, but every volley carries a visible advance glow and navigable gaps. Defeating Aurelia frees its colony and leaves the crew in a safe transition toward Act III.

    Chromatic Trench
    After the Aurelia Array falls, the jelly tendrils stretch down into Act III, the Chromatic Trench, with your lives, shields and power intact. Four new craft each teach one rule. Clawstrike snipers mark their lane with a dashed line for a moment before a fast bolt flies down it, so leave the marked lane early. Pistol shuttles lob bubbles that swell and burst into a ring of pellets; keep the bubble's edge at a distance. Angler decoys drop a glowing halo that looks like a pickup, then flash a red cross before it fires along both lines. Glassfin skate pellets glance off the trench wall once, so read the angle as well as the shot. After the vault-approach remix the Clawstrike guardian marks three lanes (five once it is enraged) and fires a bolt down each; sit in a gap between the marks. Then the trench forks, and where most of the crew flies decides the route: fly high (above the dashed channel line) for the salvage vault, four hard escalations that pay 2,500 points, a power level for every ship and the salvage itself, or fly low along the safe passage, four lighter sections that end with a shield repair. Both routes rejoin for the last eight escalations; a clean run of Act III takes about ten minutes. At the salvage vault, Lilt finds Crown-compatible hardware before B03 Clawburst arrives. Its rail shot and punch fire down the lane their dashed warning marks; after the punch the cannon cracks open and takes double damage. Free its pilot, and the Act ends quietly at the salvage dock.

    Guardians
    Midway through each Act a guardian takes the screen alone: a giant pearl urchin in Pinkwake, a giant lantern in Moonjelly Fields and a clawstrike in the Chromatic Trench. Its ring glows before every volley. The urchin fires a ring of pearls with a gap that moves round each time; the lantern fires two fans that leave the lane straight at you open. Destroy it before it escapes for a 1,500-point bonus and a power, shield and charge pickup. If the whole crew falls during its section, it returns with the continue.

    Checkpoints and continues
    Every section start and each boss is a checkpoint; a CHECKPOINT toast marks it. When the whole crew is down, a ten-second CONTINUE? countdown holds the battle still. Press A to resume from the last checkpoint with every pilot restored; the score returns to what it was at that checkpoint. Let the countdown run out to end the run. A continue during a boss fight brings the boss back as damaged as you left it, so every attempt chips away at it.

    Boss fights
    Each boss hull grows with the crew, and each extra pilot adds more than the first, so four powered-up pilots stacking supers still fight through all three phases. Phases turn at two thirds and one third of the hull. A clean solo run of each Act takes about ten minutes; a first clear with continues takes twelve to fifteen, and a crew of four finishes each in about ten to eleven.

    Future campaign
    Eight Acts are planned in total: Pinkwake, Moonjelly Fields, Chromatic Trench, Rosefin Express, Blossom Wreckyard, Crown Reef, Glass Current and Eclipse Palace, followed by the final confrontation. Acts I and II are playable today at their full 10–15-minute pacing, and so is Act III, the Chromatic Trench; its cinematics, the remaining five Acts and online co-op are still in production. This is an admin-only rehearsal, not a public leaderboard release.
