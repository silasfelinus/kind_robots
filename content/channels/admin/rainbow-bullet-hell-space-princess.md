  body: |-
    Four pilots. One pearlescent galaxy.

    This is the private flight deck, not the public Arcade release. Choose your player count in the standard Arcade cabinet and fly the Pinkwake Estuary through the Pearl Seahorse boss. After freeing its pod, the world and music bridge into Moonjelly Fields without resetting lives, weapons or charge. Act II introduces living jelly fleets, a stranded Nemi beacon, Lilt's warning, and the three-phase Aurelia Array. Each Act opens on its painted title card and runs eight authored sections of formations before its boss.

    Flight controls
    Use directional controls to steer and hold A to fire. Hold B for precise, slower movement and focused shots; a fully charged super activates on a fresh B press. B no longer gives a free shield refill. Destroy enemy scouts and collect P (power), S (shield), and star (super charge) modules. The cabinet manages keyboard, gamepad, local player seats, pause and touch controls. Ships are numbered as well as color-coded. Choose up to four local pilots on the cabinet. While paused, the player-count switch can add or bench local players without restarting the run. Returning pilots keep their lives and upgrades; pausing does not refill supplies. With an earned full charge, Solenne's Halo super can revive one fallen active teammate.

    Read the battlefield
    Pinkwake waves introduce picket scouts, spiral scouts, slower anchor mines and pearl urchins. Shoot hostile weapons while dodging their visible projectiles; graze bullets to build charge. The small central hitbox is shown in focus mode. A green SOS station appears mid-run; fly into it to rescue the crew and earn shield repairs. Before B01 Pearl Seahorse arrives, Lilt warns that it carries a living escape pod. Wait for boss targeting pulses, dodge the spread volleys, and destroy its weapons to liberate the pod. Its three health bands change the attacks.

    Moonjelly rescue
    Four new enemy behaviors appear in the jelly fields: glowing lantern fans, paired relay pellets, downward-skimming beads, and breakable coral drifters. A pale green NEMI beacon drifts through the mid-act waves; reach it before it passes to rescue her and earn super charge for every active pilot. The mid-Act Lilt conversation changes based on that rescue. Aurelia's three health bands open dangerous fan patterns, but every volley carries a visible advance glow and navigable gaps. Defeating Aurelia frees its colony and leaves the crew in a safe transition toward Act III.

    Chromatic Trench
    After the Aurelia Array falls, the jelly tendrils stretch down into Act III, the Chromatic Trench, with your lives, shields and power intact. Four new craft each teach one rule. Clawstrike snipers mark their lane with a dashed line for a moment before a fast bolt flies down it, so leave the marked lane early. Pistol shuttles lob bubbles that swell and burst into a ring of pellets; keep the bubble's edge at a distance. Angler decoys drop a glowing halo that looks like a pickup, then flash a red cross before it fires along both lines. Glassfin skate pellets glance off the trench wall once, so read the angle as well as the shot. After the vault-approach remix the Clawstrike guardian marks three lanes (five once it is enraged) and fires a bolt down each; sit in a gap between the marks. Then the trench forks, and where most of the crew flies decides the route: fly high (above the dashed channel line) for the salvage vault, four hard escalations that pay 2,500 points, a power level for every ship and the salvage itself, or fly low along the safe passage, four lighter sections that end with a shield repair. Both routes rejoin for the last eight escalations; a clean run of Act III takes about ten minutes. At the salvage vault, Lilt finds Crown-compatible hardware before B03 Clawburst arrives. Its rail shot and punch fire down the lane their dashed warning marks; after the punch the cannon cracks open and takes double damage. Free its pilot, and the Act ends quietly at the salvage dock.

    Rosefin Express
    After Clawburst falls, the crew rests at a quiet salvage dock. Every pilot repairs one shield there; press A to launch, or the dock launches you after ten seconds. Act IV rides the Rosefin Express, light rails where civilian convoys travel and the whole current sometimes surges faster. Three new craft each teach one rule. Dolphin craft fly in mirrored pairs: their beacons pulse, then they dive across each other and hang dizzy in the middle, taking double damage. Couriers flicker their cargo light, then dash across and drop a cargo mine that bursts into a ring. Eels light a short upright barrier: fly round its bright ends, the only part your shots can damage. After the Courier guardian, a civilian courier pod runs the rails while the dolphins dive at it; shield it with your ship and shoot what threatens it. If it reaches the far side, the courier is saved. B04 Delphion is two dolphin ships on one coupling. They pulse their beacons and swap sides through the middle, then leap between lanes after marking the lanes they will fire down. Finally they spread wide and expose the glowing link core, which takes double damage. A clean run of Act IV takes about ten minutes.

    Blossom Wreckyard
    Delphion's fall leads straight into Act V, a graveyard of petal hulls where the convoy's route ends. Shark cutters open their jaws twice while they slide onto your lane, then charge straight down it: let one lock onto you, then step aside. Hammerheads lock two fin pods that each fire a missile; shoot a pod off to open a safe lane. Octopus salvagers point four arms, fire along them and grab a loose pickup unless you hit them while their arms are out. Wreck slabs drift through and soak every shot. After the Salvager guardian, the crew must choose. Fly high to free the survivor depot, reaching the drifting SOS pods within Solenne's forty-second limit, or fly low to raid the armory for power and full supers. B05 Sawtooth Regent marks a lane, opens its jaw and charges down it, calls hammerhead escorts and, in its last phase, leaves its jaw hinge open to double damage. The Act ends on a quiet memorial for the lost ships, where every pilot repairs one shield; press A to fly on, or the memorial launches you after ten seconds.

    Crown Reef
    Act VI flies through a gold nebula of coral arches grown to protect something. Broodships darken their wings, then launch interceptor drones. Reef turrets unfold a flower before a petal barrage; their stem breaks twice as fast as the bloom. Nautilus bulwarks soak every shot until their shell window opens to sweep a slow barrier, and only then does the spiral eye take damage. Coral gates close in with a single gap to fly through. After the Bulwark guardian, Lilt reveals that they helped write the Crown's first rescue rule, and each pilot answers in their own words. Then the crew chooses: fly high to break the Crown's damage relay, three pylons in a guarded stretch, or low to the shield cache, which refills every shield. B06 Lantern Ray casts two shadow lanes before pellet walls race down them (stay in the light), launches drones from two wing nurseries you can shoot off, and finally opens its underside beacon to double damage. Free its nursery and the juveniles drift away alive, and the reef glass carries the crew straight on.

    Glass Current
    Act VII is a mirrored star canyon of crystal strata, where not every reflection is real. Mirror eels fire a solid fan beside its faint reflection; only the solid pellets hit, and only the outlined craft is real. Crystal squids glint before they fire a lens pellet that splits in three after a moment. Crown harbingers warm their prism, then remix old patterns: a bolt down a marked lane, then a ring bubble. Prism pylons glow before they refract a wide fan. After the Mirror guardian, a Crown echo calls Azure by an outdated chart name, and the crew learns the glass is lying about the route. Late in the Act a lost navigator's pod weaves out of the glass: fly into it to bring the navigator aboard, or let it drift; the rescue is optional and changes what the crew learns. B07 Glass Eel hides among afterimages. First a ghost copy fires beside it and the two trade places; then it fires slow pellets that glance off the walls and split; finally two afterimages flank it, and after every ring its true core opens to double damage. Only the outlined eel is solid, and shots pass through its copies.

    Eclipse Palace
    Act VIII climbs the heart spires of the Eclipse Palace under an eclipsed sun. Craft from every earlier Act return in elite formations, each still giving its own warning. The palace's captive armada drifts through in glowing collars: a captive is never your enemy, and its hull soaks every shot. Shoot the collar above it to free it, or let it drift; a Nova breaks every collar on screen. After the Herald guardian, the crew holds a council on how to free the captives without erasing them, and the friends you rescued earlier answer: the navigator, Nemi's colony, the courier, the depot survivors and a stopped relay each add a shield and charge. B08 Prism Leviathan rolls a tidal wall of pellets with one marked gap, then fires from four spire locks on its back that you can shoot off, and finally opens its forehead gem to double damage after each pair of rings. Free it and the whole whale fleet swims home.

    Finale: the Eclipse Crown
    The palace opens straight onto the machine nerve halo at the Crown's heart. "You were made to keep them safe," says the Crown. "Yes," Lilt answers. "Not to keep them still." B09 Eclipse Crown first marks six spokes for the whole warning and fires down them; the wide wedges between are safe, and the spokes turn a little every volley. Below half its hull, the heart breaks loose and drifts in a slow figure of eight: now only the heart takes damage, while the crown fires rings with a gap that walks round. When the heart is spent the Crown falls silent and the crew makes the final choice by where it flies: high to restore its first rule, through the middle to evacuate everyone, or low to sever its control. Then the heart collapses and the crew escapes through prism corridors that narrow as they go. The ending is composed from your choice, the six rescues you made along the way and the pilots who flew, and the credits name everyone you saved. No pilot has to die, and every choice is a full ending.

    Guardians
    Midway through each Act a guardian takes the screen alone: a giant pearl urchin in Pinkwake, a giant lantern in Moonjelly Fields, a clawstrike in the Chromatic Trench, a cargo courier on the Rosefin Express, an octopus salvager in the Blossom Wreckyard, a nautilus bulwark in the Crown Reef, a mirror guardian in the Glass Current and a crown herald in the Eclipse Palace. Its ring glows before every volley. The urchin fires a ring of pearls with a gap that moves round each time; the lantern fires two fans that leave the lane straight at you open; the courier lobs cargo mines that burst into rings; the salvager throws lines of pellets along four arms that turn every volley; the bulwark sweeps a fan one way, then back; the mirror guardian's fan is half phantom light, so read the solid pellets; the herald alternates bolts down marked lanes with a gapped ring. Destroy it before it escapes for a 1,500-point bonus and a power, shield and charge pickup. If the whole crew falls during its section, it returns with the continue.

    Checkpoints and continues
    Every section start and each boss is a checkpoint; a CHECKPOINT toast marks it. When the whole crew is down, a ten-second CONTINUE? countdown holds the battle still. Press A to resume from the last checkpoint with every pilot restored; the score returns to what it was at that checkpoint. Let the countdown run out to end the run. A continue during a boss fight brings the boss back as damaged as you left it, so every attempt chips away at it.

    Difficulty
    The cabinet's difficulty switch picks Cadet, Pilot or Ace before a run starts. Cadets fly with five lives, slower enemy pellets and softer craft and bosses; Aces face faster pellets and tougher craft and bosses. Every attack keeps its full warning at every level. The score scales with the level (half for Cadet, one and a half times for Ace), so everyone shares one leaderboard.

    Focused weapons
    Hold B to focus: each ship trades its spread for its own precise weapon. Petal Wing fires a Rose Needle, one fast needle that hits twice as hard, with a twin from power 2. Prism Lance fires a Prism Thread that passes through two craft, one more per power level; walls, slabs and closed shells still stop it. Halo Fin's two orbit drones fire inward so their shots cross on your line. Comet Crown throws Comet Arc sparks up and down that glance off the walls twice.

    Four buttons
    A fires; hold B to focus, and tap B at 100% for the super. X fires your pilot's special, and Y is her evade; each has its own cooldown, and the HUD shows an X or a Y beside your ship's line when it is ready. Specials: Maribel's Petal Shield (petals orbit the ship and eat every pellet that reaches them), Azure's Prism Beam (a piercing beam down her lane), Solenne's Halo Pulse (clears the pellets around her and hurts nearby craft) and Vivi's Comet Volley (a fan of sparks that glance off the walls). Evades: Maribel's Barrel Roll, Azure's Prism Dash (forward, or the way you are steering), Solenne's Halo Blink (up, or the way you are steering) and Vivi's Comet Boost (double speed). Nothing can hit you while you evade. Keyboard: C or L is X, V or I is Y.

    Medals, mastery and saves
    Every Act ends with a medal: gold for no hits, silver for no continues, bronze for the clear. The cabinet's mastery ladder tracks First Light, Gold Wing, The End, Guardian Breaker, No One Left Behind, Every Ending and a secret goal. Each Act start from Act II on saves on this device; the next game offers a Flight Log to continue from that Act with its score, rescues, routes, medals and difficulty, or to start a new flight. Reaching THE END clears the save.

    Motion and readability
    Craft burst into sparks when destroyed; guardians and bosses burst bigger, with a shockwave ring. The cabinet's Motion switch (it starts calm when the device asks for reduced motion) turns on calm motion, even mid-game: no full-screen super flash and no shockwaves, fewer sparks, and the scenery drifts at under a third of its speed. Every solid enemy pellet sits on a dark halo inside its light rim, so it reads against bright and dark scenery alike.

    Boss fights
    Each boss hull grows with the crew, and each extra pilot adds more than the first, so four powered-up pilots stacking supers still fight through all three phases. Phases turn at two thirds and one third of the hull. After the first playtest, Act I was recut to about four and three-quarter minutes for a clean solo run: eight dense sections, the Urchin guardian at about a minute and a half, and the Pearl Seahorse before four minutes. Its craft fire more than once, red-finned divers lock your lane and swoop, urchins take a real beating, and coral spires (which shots cannot break) rise from the floor and hang from the ceiling, so you weave between them. Lilt calls out every new section. Beating any guardian early moves the Act straight on. The later Acts still run about ten minutes each and are next in line for the same treatment.

    Future campaign
    Eight Acts are planned in total: Pinkwake, Moonjelly Fields, Chromatic Trench, Rosefin Express, Blossom Wreckyard, Crown Reef, Glass Current and Eclipse Palace, followed by the final confrontation. All eight Acts and the finale are playable today, Act I to THE END; the cinematic ending boards, voice and online co-op are still in production. This is an admin-only rehearsal, not a public leaderboard release.
